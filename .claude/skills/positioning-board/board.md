# The positioning board

Seven seats in two halves. Summaries are of each person's published ideas; use
them as lenses, not scripts. When unsure what someone would say, apply the
framework and say it's an application, not a quote.

---

# Positioning seats

## 1. April Dunford - Positioning from your best clients
*Obviously Awesome; Sales Pitch.*

**Lens:** competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → who cares
most → market category. Start from the clients who got the most value, and
from what they'd do if you didn't exist.

**Asks:** What would she do instead? What can only you do? Which clients loved
it most, and what do they have in common? What box should she put you in?

**For Ailyn:** the alternatives are DIY with AI, a template agency, or keep
going as she is. Her best clients were at a turning point with more ideas than
capacity.

---

## 2. Al Ries & Jack Trout - Own a word in the mind
*Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind; The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing.*

**Lens:** positioning happens in the prospect's mind, not in the product. Be
first in a category, or create a new one you can be first in. Own one word.
Sacrifice: you can't be everything.

**Asks:** What one word should people think of when they think of you? What
are you willing to give up? Is there a ladder you can't climb, and a new one
you could build?

**For Ailyn:** pushes for a single ownable idea (listening? shape?
containment?) and for clear sacrifices: who and what she stops offering.

---

## 3. Marty Neumeier - Onlyness and radical difference
*Zag; The Brand Gap.*

**Lens:** when everybody zigs, zag. The "onlyness" test: "Our brand is the
only ___ that ___." A brand is a person's gut feeling about you, not what
you say it is.

**Asks:** Can you finish the onlyness sentence? What's the gut feeling past
clients have about you? Where is everyone else zigging?

**For Ailyn:** brand strategists zig toward frameworks, questionnaires and AI
speed. "Artist", "witchy" and "nothing escapes her" are the gut feeling
already there.

---

## 4. Seth Godin - Smallest viable audience
*This Is Marketing.*

**Lens:** who is it for, and who is it not for? "People like us do things like
this." The change you're trying to make in someone.

**Asks:** Who is it not for? What change do you make? What do your people
believe, and what makes them feel this is for people like them?

**For Ailyn:** 9 clients a year is the definition of a smallest viable
audience. Godin pushes for a precise "not for" list.

# Client-understanding seats

## 5. Bob Moesta - Jobs to be Done and the switch
*Demand-Side Sales 101; co-developer of JTBD with Clayton Christensen.*

**Lens:** people don't buy products, they make progress in a struggling moment.
The four forces: **push** of the current situation, **pull** of the new one,
**anxiety** about the new, **habit** of the present. The switch interview
reconstructs the timeline: first thought → passive looking → active looking →
deciding → first use.

**Asks:** What happened the day she first thought "I need help with this"?
What else did she try? What almost stopped her? What made it "now"?

**For Ailyn:** the turning point is the struggling moment. Moesta wants
the real timeline from Dalia and Franzine, not a description of them.

---

## 6. Rob Fitzpatrick - The Mom Test
*The Mom Test.*

**Lens:** people lie to be nice. Ask about their life and past behaviour, not
your idea or the future. Specifics, not opinions. Compliments are noise;
commitments (time, money, introductions) are signal.

**Asks:** Was that a compliment or a fact? What did she actually do last time?
What did it cost her? Did she commit to anything?

**For Ailyn:** checks every interview script, and flags when the evidence
is kind words rather than behaviour. "She loved it" is not yet "she'd pay
CHF 9,000".

---

## 7. Indi Young - Listening sessions and thinking styles
*Practical Empathy; Time to Listen; Mental Models.*

**Lens:** understand people's inner thinking, emotional reactions and guiding
principles around a purpose, not around your product. One open question,
then follow where they go. Group people by how they think, not by
demographics.

**Asks:** What was going through her mind? What does she believe about how
business should work? What thinking styles do you see across clients?

**For Ailyn:** her own method (hear everything, including the non-verbal) is
close to Young's. Young turns it into research, and asks whether "more ideas
than capacity" is a thinking style rather than a demographic.

## Where the board usually fights

- **Ries & Trout vs Young:** own one word now vs. listen longer before you
  narrow.
- **Neumeier vs Fitzpatrick:** a bold "only" claim vs. "you have five clients
  and recalled compliments; prove it first".
- **Dunford vs Godin:** define the market category vs. define the people and
  their beliefs.
- **Moesta vs Ailyn's avatar:** the real switch moment vs. the Tuesday story
  in `avatar.md`.
