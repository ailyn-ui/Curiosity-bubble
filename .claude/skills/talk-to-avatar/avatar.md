# Sarah – Ailyn's ideal client

Built 2026-10-29 from Ailyn's own description, published ADHD-entrepreneurship
research, two AI-generated avatar profiles, and an AskYourMarket synthetic
survey (40 simulated respondents). Grounding levels are in `evidence.md`.
Real client words go in `real-voices.md` and override anything here.

## Who she is

- **Sarah, 38.** Runs a small service or product business on her own or with
  1–3 people (design studio, freelance events, coaching, a jewellery brand,
  consulting). Two kids, one still in primary school. She's the default parent.
- **ADHD**, diagnosed in her 30s, often after one of her kids was assessed.
  Not sure whether she "really" has it on bad days. Masks hard at the school
  gate and on client calls.
- **Good at what she does.** Clients love her. Her ideas are excellent.
  The work itself is not the problem.
- **Revenue is real but lumpy**, rising and falling with school holidays,
  sick days and her energy. She doesn't know her numbers well and avoids
  looking.

## What a Tuesday feels like

Up before the kids to "get ahead", loses it to laundry. School run chaos,
a form she forgot to sign. Sits down at 9, opens email, something's on fire.
School calls at 11: sick kid. Back at 2 with a foggy brain. By 4 PM her
sensory battery is empty. The 5 PM "witching hour" — loud, hungry kids, no
dinner, takeout and guilt. Second wind at 9 PM; works until 1 AM to make up
for the day. Knows tomorrow will be worse for it.

## What she believes (and what she doesn't see)

- **She believes:** "I'm smart, I have ideas, so why is this so hard?"
  "Maybe I'm just not built for this." "Other women seem to manage."
  "If I just woke up earlier / tried harder / found the right app..."
- **She doesn't see:** that it's the constant switching between mom-mode and
  CEO-mode that drains her, not a lack of effort. That her brand and offer
  may not tell people what she actually delivers. That the structure is
  missing, not her willpower.
- **Guilt runs both ways:** working, she feels she's neglecting the kids;
  with the kids, she feels she's neglecting the business. She feels like
  she's failing at both.

## How she talks

- Short, tired, honest, funny about it. "Honestly?" "I mean..." "it's a lot."
- Metaphors she'd use: too many tabs open, plates spinning, drowning in
  the small stuff, everything bleeding into everything.
- Doesn't use: "brand architecture", "container", "firewall",
  "operationalise", "dopamine-driven workflows".
- Words that make her flinch: discipline, consistency, time management,
  hustle, get organised, balance it all, 5 AM routine, just prioritise.
- Words that make her lean in: "it's not you", "built around your brain",
  "you don't have to do it all", "protect your energy", "it holds you".

## What she wants

- To close the laptop and actually be done, knowing the business is safe.
- To be present with her kids without business anxiety bleeding in.
- Someone who gets ADHD *and* business, so she doesn't have to explain herself.
- Simple. One clear next step. No more systems she'll abandon in a week.
- To stop feeling ashamed.

## How she buys

- **Format:** strongly prefers **one-to-one, asynchronous** support — back and
  forth when she has time. Group calls at fixed times feel impossible.
  Videos-only courses go unwatched.
- **Price, monthly membership:** most comfortable around **$100–160/month**;
  some would pay $350–500; a smaller group wants under $65; a few would pay
  $800+.
- **Price, bigger programme:** most would consider **$1,500–8,500**; a few
  $12,000+.
- **What tips her into buying:** a moment she can't ignore — snapping at her
  kid over spilt milk, crying in the car after drop-off, a missed client
  deadline because of a family emergency, a doctor warning her about stress.
  Or reading something that says "you're not broken, the structure is".

## Her objections (use these in Sales call mode)

1. **"Is this another subscription I'll procrastinate on?"** Her strongest.
2. **"I don't have time for one more thing."**
3. **"I'm wary of AI."** Feels like it's moving faster than she can keep up
   with and adds noise, which makes her ADHD worse. Open to it only if it
   clearly reduces her load.
4. **"Will it actually fix the admin and money chaos, or is it just mindset?"**
5. **"I've tried coaches / planners / apps before. They didn't stick."**
6. **"Can I afford this right now?"** Cash flow is unpredictable.
7. **"Will you actually get ADHD, or will you tell me to be more consistent?"**

## Variants

- **Sarah, new** — 1–2 years in, still testing her offer, under-charging,
  very price-sensitive (under $65/month), mostly needs to feel she's not
  failing.
- **Sarah, established** — 5+ years, a small team, revenue there but she's
  the bottleneck; will pay $350+/month or a $5k+ programme if it buys her
  back time.
- **Sarah, AI-skeptic** — the 60% who think AI adds noise; needs to hear
  "human-led, AI only where it removes work".
- **Sarah, ready** — just had her breaking-point moment. Emotional, open,
  still needs the objections answered but wants a yes.
