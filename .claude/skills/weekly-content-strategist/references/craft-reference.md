# Craft reference: evolve a winner, never rerun the topic

The condensed knowledge behind the memo. Read it before building the ideas tables. Principles only; the tools and budgets live in `weekly-run.md`, the exact tables in `ideas-memo-format.md`.

## Why evolve instead of repeat

A winner proves two things: the audience wants the claim, and this creator can land it. Repeating the topic spends that proof. Evolving it compounds it: same claim, one step deeper or sideways, so regular readers get something new and new readers still meet the idea. Every idea in the memo should feel like the next chapter of the creator's own thinking, never market news adapted to them.

## The eleven evolve rules

1. **Keep the claim, change exactly one dimension.** Angle, depth, format, audience stage, or scale. One change per idea. Change two and it stops being a sequel; change none and it is a rerun.
2. **Climb the ladder.** Every claim has rungs: the claim, why it is true, how to do it, where it breaks, who it fails for, what it costs, the opposite, the story of learning it. Find the rungs already published (the Topic ledger) and take the next empty one. The biggest long-form winner always gets a sequel one rung down.
3. **Answer what they asked next.** The top question or pushback in the replies of a winner is the next post. The audience already wrote the brief.
4. **Get more specific, never more general.** A winner about "pricing" evolves into "the first invoice", never into "business". Specific beats broad every time on every platform.
5. **Cross it with a second pillar.** Take the winning claim from pillar A and run it through pillar B. The overlap is where a creator's angle lives.
6. **Borrow the market's structure, never its topic.** A finding gives a sequence of moves, a pacing, a hook mechanism. Take that and pour the creator's own claim into it. Never reuse a run of 8 or more words from any receipt post.
7. **Contradict yourself on purpose.** Take a claim they made and argue the case where it is wrong. Honest, specific, with the condition named. This is the sharpest sequel shape and the one most creators never use.
8. **Name it.** When a claim keeps working, give it a name and add the name to the Vocabulary section of the throughline. Named ideas travel, get quoted, and get asked about.
9. **The sameness test.** Read the idea next to the winner it came from. If a regular reader would say "you already posted this", it fails. Fix the dimension, the rung, or the specificity until it passes.
10. **Word-for-word reruns, tightly capped.** Allowed only for a top-decile winner that is 90 or more days old. Label the row "rerun" in From. At most 2 of the 10 short-form rows. Reruns never count as new ideas.
11. **Cross a library seed with a winner.** A saved highlight, note, or capture that agrees with a winner becomes a sequel with fresh material. One that clashes becomes a contrarian post. Name the item in From.

The From column names the source and the rule for every row. No exceptions. A row that cannot name both gets cut.

## How the lanes turn into rows

- **Scoreboard rows** (rows 1 to 3 of short-form, minimum): the creator's own winners, evolved by rules 1 to 5, 7, or 8. Judge winners by typical multiple, never one hit. 2x is solid, 3x and up a real breakout.
- **Finding rows**: each finding feeds at least one row through rule 6. The finding decides the structure; the throughline decides the substance. Own posts are never findings.
- **Library rows** (at least two when seeds exist): rule 11. A seed the creator saved but never published is the freshest material they own.
- **Long-form rows**: the sequel to the biggest long-form winner (rule 2), at least one library-built piece (rule 11), and each piece names the short-form rows it feeds so the week compounds.

## Pillar and hook discipline

- Every idea passes the throughline's lens: would this creator say this, in their words, to their reader, against their enemy. If not, cut it, however strong the receipt.
- Vary hook shapes across the table: a number, a confession, a contrarian claim, a question the audience asked, a specific moment, a rule, a list. Two rows sharing shape and pillar means one goes.
- Openers use the creator's own vocabulary from the throughline and their winners. A hook that could belong to any growth account is a failed hook.
- Titles and claims get no colons and no headline punctuation.

## Grading last week

One honest line per pick. Posted and beat baseline: say by how much. Posted and under: say it and name the likely reason (rung, format, hook shape). Not posted: "You did not post it." Grades feed the Topic ledger and the Stop doing section. A soft grade makes next week's list worse.

## Throughline upkeep

The throughline changes only when earned: a format proven three times, a pillar the numbers say is dead, vocabulary the audience adopted, a lesson for Do not do. Propose exact edits, apply after a yes. The Topic ledger always updates with the recommended angles and what got published. Every change gets a dated Change log line. Keep the note under 800 words and the ledger under 30 topics.

## Writing rules

Plain spoken, short. No em dashes. No analyst jargon (salience, narrative, paradigm, discourse, reframe as a noun). Banned AI frames at most once per memo. Never coin a shorthand for a finding. Post ids never in prose. Never diagnose a medical or mental-health condition for a hook. Never invent posts, creators, metrics, links, or library items. Say which lines are source-backed and which are inference. Thin evidence still gets ten short-form rows, built from own winners and seeds, with the light rows labeled.
