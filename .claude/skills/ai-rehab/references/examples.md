# Before and after examples

These are illustrations. The drafts are made up for teaching, and every number on a card came from running `count_tells.py` on the text shown. Follow the draft in front of you, not the wording of these rewrites.

## Contents

1. LinkedIn post, deep depth, main point kept, "show changes"
2. Client email, standard depth, courtesy kept
3. A mostly human note, light depth
4. Fine print with a condition, and the card when a limit is lost
5. Email newsletter, standard depth, full card

## 1. LinkedIn post, deep depth, main point kept, "show changes"

Before:

```text
Here's the thing — most clinic owners don't have a marketing problem. They have a follow-up problem. 🦷

Last quarter we added an AI employee to our front desk. It's not just a chatbot — it's a game-changer for patient care.

It confirms appointments, sends reminders, and follows up on unbooked treatment plans, seamlessly saving our team 12 hours a week.

✅ Fewer no-shows
✅ Fuller schedule
✅ Happier team

The bottom line: we booked 31 extra cleanings in September.

Ready to transform your practice? Let that sink in.

#DentalMarketing #PracticeGrowth
```

After (deep depth rewrites the whole post from its claims, in the draft's tone):

```text
For most clinic owners, the real problem is follow-up, not marketing. 🦷

Last quarter we put an AI employee on our front desk. It confirms appointments, sends reminders, and follows up on the treatment plans that never got booked, which saves our team 12 hours a week. We've had fewer no-shows, a fuller schedule, and a happier team, and in September that meant 31 extra cleanings.

#DentalMarketing #PracticeGrowth
```

Card, after the user said "show changes":

```text
AI Rehab: 12 tells fixed (11 found by script, 1 by reading). Facts kept (3 items checked by script). Warmth kept (6 before, 5 after by script, from the cut bait question).
Only you can add or fix: your next step (the bait question was the only ask)
Say "drop hashtags" to remove them.

What changed
- The post was rewritten from its claims. Each sentence carries a claim from the draft, and no claim is new.
- "Here's the thing" opener cut.
- "don't have a marketing problem. They have a follow-up problem." (a "not X" line, found by reading) is the post's main point, so both halves stay, in one plain sentence.
- "It's not just a chatbot, it's a game-changer" cut. The next sentence already says what it does.
- "seamlessly" cut, and "unbooked treatment plans" became "treatment plans that never got booked" (same meaning, plainer).
- The three checkmark bullets became one sentence with the same three claims.
- "The bottom line:" label removed. "that meant" keeps the link between the AI employee and the 31 cleanings, and the post now ends on that result.
- "Ready to transform your practice? Let that sink in." cut (engagement bait).
- Tooth emoji and hashtags kept.

Kept on purpose
- Nothing.
```

What to notice: the "not X" line that carries the post's point survives, and the one that was only a runway ("not just a chatbot") goes. The compare listed a lost "not" and a new "never": the "not" sat in the cut runway line, and "never" came from rewording "unbooked". No claim changed.

## 2. Client email, standard depth, courtesy kept

Before:

```text
Hi Marco,

I hope this email finds you well! Thanks so much for making time to chat on Thursday — it was truly a pleasure learning about Rossi Landscaping.

I wanted to follow up on our conversation regarding monthly bookkeeping. As discussed, our comprehensive package would be $850/month, starting November 1, and includes reconciliation, payroll support, and a monthly profit report. This would empower you to streamline your finances and focus on what you do best.

Should you have any questions, please don't hesitate to let me know. I'd be more than happy to walk you through the details.

Looking forward to hearing from you!

Best,
Dana
```

After (standard depth keeps the email's shape and rewrites each sentence in Dana's tone):

```text
Hi Marco,

Thanks so much for making time to chat on Thursday. I enjoyed learning about Rossi Landscaping!

The monthly bookkeeping package we talked about would be $850/month, starting November 1. It covers reconciliation, payroll support, and a monthly profit report, so your finances would be simpler and you'd have more time for the work you do best.

If you have any questions, I'd be happy to walk you through the details.

Looking forward to hearing from you!

Best,
Dana
```

Card (under 150 words, so one line):

```text
AI Rehab: 8 tells fixed (5 found by script, 3 by reading). Facts kept (6 items checked by script). Warmth kept (20 before, 13 after by script, from the cut opener and canned lines).
```

What to notice: the greeting, thanks, offer, questions line, close, and sign-off are all still there, in the same order. The opening thanks already sounded like Dana, so it stayed. The reader found three canned lines the script missed: the "finds you well" opener, the "I wanted to follow up" wind-up, and the "don't hesitate" sign-off. The sign-off became the same offer in plain words, merged with Dana's offer to walk through the details, and it names no new way to get in touch. The price keeps the draft's "would be", since Marco hasn't said yes yet, and "would be simpler" keeps the benefit a maybe.

## 3. A mostly human note, light depth

Before and after (nothing changed):

```text
We sold out of sourdough by 9am on Saturday, which has never happened in four years. My mom came in to help at the register and kept telling every single customer that the starter is older than me (it's true, it turns 31 in November). A guy from the hardware store down the street bought the last loaf and then came back an hour later with coffee for the whole crew. I teared up a little — this town shows up for the people who show up for it. We're baking a double batch next weekend, so come early or text us and we'll hold one with your name on it.
```

Card:

```text
AI Rehab: 1 tell found by script and kept as your voice (light touch). Facts kept (5 items checked by script).
```

What to notice: it reads like a person wrote it, so the depth is light. The writer's one dash stays, and so does everything else.

## 4. Fine print with a condition, and the card when a limit is lost

Before:

```text
Our coaching program comes with a 30-day guarantee — refunds are available only if you've attended at least 2 sessions. We truly stand behind our work. Monthly plans of 4 x $250 are available for clients in the US and Canada.
```

After:

```text
Our coaching program comes with a 30-day guarantee. Refunds are available only if you've attended at least 2 sessions. We stand behind our work. Monthly plans of 4 x $250 are available for clients in the US and Canada.
```

Card:

```text
AI Rehab: 2 tells fixed (found by script). Facts kept (5 items checked by script). Warmth unchanged.
```

A rewrite that dropped the limit:

```text
Our coaching program comes with a 30-day guarantee. Refunds are available if you've attended at least 2 sessions. We stand behind our work. Monthly plans of 4 x $250 are available for clients in the US and Canada.
```

Card if the limit could not be restored:

```text
Needs your eyes: "only if you've attended at least 2 sessions" lost the word "only".
```

What to notice: the compare lists the lost "only" with its sentence, and the claim re-read fails. The fix is to put "only" back and run the check again. The "Needs your eyes" card is for a loss you could not fix.

## 5. Email newsletter, standard depth, full card

Before:

```text
Subject: Big News From Tidewater Tailoring

Hi [First Name],

We're over the moon to share that Tidewater Tailoring now offers same-day hemming! 🎉 As a family-run shop, we truly know how stressful it is when your pants don't fit the morning of a big event. Now you can drop them off in the morning and wear them that evening.

Here's how it works:
- Drop off by 10am, Monday through Friday.
- Pick up after 4pm the same day.
- Hemming is $18 per pair, and cuffs are $6 extra.

Same-day service covers hems only. Suits, dresses, and anything that needs a fitting still take our usual 5 to 7 days, because we want every alteration done right.

It's not just about speed — it's about giving you one less thing to worry about before the moments that matter.

Questions? Reply to this email or call us at (843) 555-0142. [Insert holiday hours]

Thanks for supporting a local shop.

Warmly,
Rosa
```

After (standard depth keeps the sections, the list, and the order of points):

```text
Subject: Same-day hemming at Tidewater Tailoring

Hi [First Name],

Tidewater Tailoring now offers same-day hemming! 🎉 We're a family-run shop, and we know how stressful it is when your pants don't fit the morning of a big event. Now you can drop them off in the morning and wear them that evening.

- Drop off by 10am, Monday through Friday.
- Pick up after 4pm the same day.
- Hemming is $18 per pair, and cuffs are $6 extra.

Same-day service covers hems only. Suits, dresses, and anything that needs a fitting still take our usual 5 to 7 days, because we want every alteration done right. Same-day hemming is one less thing to worry about before a big day.

Questions? Reply to this email or call us at (843) 555-0142. [Insert holiday hours]

Thanks for supporting a local shop.

Warmly,
Rosa
```

Card:

```text
AI Rehab report
Tells: 7 found (4 by script, 3 by reading), 6 fixed, 1 kept on purpose
Facts: 13 items checked, all kept, nothing new (counted by script). Claims: 14 of 14 kept (re-read)
Warmth and courtesy: kept (12 signals before, 10 after, from the cut announcement line and runway line, tone re-read)
Voice: standard (rewritten from your facts, in your draft's tone)
Channel: email newsletter (my guess). Depth: standard
Only you can add or fix: your holiday hours ("[Insert holiday hours]")
Say "show changes", "lighter", "deeper", or "redo for email"
```

What to notice: the announcement frame went and the news leads. "family-run" and "same-day" describe the shop and the offer, so they stay. The limits ("hems only", "5 to 7 days") and the reason for them stay word for word. The "not just about speed" line was a runway, not the newsletter's point, so its claim stays in plain words and the candle line ("the moments that matter") goes. The merge field "[First Name]" stays because the email tool fills it. "[Insert holiday hours]" stays because only Rosa knows the hours, so it is kept on purpose and listed for her. The Title Case subject line became sentence case, and the shop's name kept its capitals.
