# Scoring Rubric

Use these anchors so scores stay consistent from post to post. The examples span different industries on purpose. Translate them to the user's niche using their profile.

## The scale

| Score | Means |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | Generic. She's scrolled past this exact idea a hundred times. Could be posted by anyone in the niche. |
| 4-6 | Decent and forgettable. Accurate, maybe even useful, but nothing she'd stop for, save, or send. |
| 7-8 | Strong. Specific to her, teaches or names something real, earns the stop. |
| 9-10 | The best thing in her feed today. Rare. She saves it, sends it, and remembers who posted it. |

When you're between two scores, take the lower one.

---

## 1. Stops the scroll

What you're scoring: the first line of a caption or text post, slide 1 of a carousel, the first two seconds (spoken and on-screen) of a video, frame 1 of a story sequence.

**Scores high when the hook:**
- names the reader, her situation, or her exact words in the first few words
- is specific enough to feel like it was written for her (numbers, names, a concrete moment)
- opens a loop the post fully closes
- could only have been written by someone who knows this audience

**Scores low when the hook:**
- is a generic truth ("Consistency is key.")
- opens with the creator ("So excited to share...", "I've been thinking a lot about...")
- is clickbait the post doesn't pay off
- warms up for two sentences before saying anything

**Examples:**
- 2/10: "Let's talk about pricing!"
- 5/10: "Most coaches undercharge. Here's why."
- 8/10: "If you raised your prices last year and still made less, your pricing isn't the problem. Your package is."

---

## 2. Most valuable thing she saw today

**Scores high when she learns something concrete:** a mechanism (why the problem happens), a named mistake, a real number, a step-by-step she can use today, or a reframe that changes how she sees the problem.

**Scores low when it's:** advice she's heard everywhere ("post consistently," "drink more water," "know your numbers"), a restated platitude, or motivation with no method.

**Examples:**
- Realtor, 3/10: "Staging your home helps it sell faster!"
- Realtor, 8/10: "Buyers decide in the first 8 photos. Here are the 3 rooms that belong in those slots and the one that should never be photo #1."
- Bookkeeper, 3/10: "Keep your business and personal expenses separate."
- Bookkeeper, 8/10: "If your restaurant's food cost is over 32%, check these three line items before you blame your suppliers."

---

## 3. She'd send it to a friend

**Scores high when:** it names a shared experience so precisely that forwarding it says "this is us." Usually an exact moment, an inner monologue, or a mistake everyone in her world makes and nobody admits.

**Scores low when:** it's useful but impersonal. Nothing makes her think of one specific person.

**The test:** can you picture who she sends it to and what she types? ("lol this is literally you," "we need to do this.") If not, it's a 6 or lower.

---

## 4. Guilty it was free

**Scores high when:** it over-delivers. The full what and the how, a complete thought she can act on, dense enough that she'd have paid for it.

**Scores low when:** the payoff is withheld ("DM me for the rest," "comment GUIDE"), it skims the surface and stops before the useful part, or filler stretches a thin idea.

**Note:** a comment-for-a-freebie CTA doesn't automatically fail this filter. It fails only if the post itself gave nothing complete before the ask.

---

## 5. The reader is the hero

**Scores high when:** the post centers "you," her experience, her frustration, her win. Creator stories and client stories turn the spotlight back on her ("If that's where you are, here's what I'd do first").

**Scores low when:** the creator is the hero ("I did X, then I achieved Y"), the product is the hero, or it's a personal update with no bridge back to her.

**The strip test:** take the reader out entirely. If the post still works as written, she isn't the hero. Score 5 or lower.

---

## 6. Valuable even if she never buys

**Scores high when:** a complete, usable takeaway lands before any mention of the offer, and the CTA is an optional next step.

**Scores low when:** the post is an ad in disguise, the value only exists after purchase, or everything builds toward "so buy this."

**The test:** delete the last two lines and the offer mention. Is it still worth reading? If not, score 4 or lower.

---

## 7. The CTA is earned

Score N/A if there's no CTA, and add one line on whether one would help (a post driving to a launch probably needs one, a pure value or story post often doesn't).

**Scores high when:** there's one ask, it follows naturally from the value, the effort it asks for matches what she just got (a small ask after one tip, a bigger ask after a full framework), and it's specific ("Comment AUDIT and I'll send the checklist").

**Scores low when:** there are several asks competing ("like, save, share, follow, and DM me"), the ask comes out of nowhere, it's vague ("Let me know your thoughts!"), it's engagement bait ("Tag someone who needs this!"), or it asks for a big commitment after thin value.

---

## Finding the weakest line

After scoring, find the one sentence costing the most points. It's usually one of these:
- the hook (most common)
- a generic sentence where a specific one belongs
- the line where the payoff gets withheld
- the sentence where the post turns from her story to the creator's
- the CTA

Quote it exactly. Say in one line what it costs and which filter it hurts.
