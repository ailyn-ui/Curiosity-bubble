# Format Notes

The seven filters apply to every format, but where each filter lives changes. Read the section for the format you're scoring.

## Caption (Instagram, Facebook, single image or photo post)

- **Hook** = the first line before "...more." Roughly 125 characters show. If the hook isn't in that window, it doesn't exist.
- The image or graphic has to earn the stop too. If the user describes it, score it as part of the hook.
- Break long captions into short paragraphs, and keep some sentences together so it doesn't turn into a wall of one-liners.
- Hashtags don't count toward value. Ignore them unless they crowd the caption.

## Carousel (Instagram, LinkedIn document posts)

- **Hook** = slide 1. It needs a clear promise readable in about two seconds, so large text and few words.
- One idea per slide. A slide that tries to hold two ideas usually loses both.
- Each slide should make her swipe to the next one. A dead slide in the middle loses readers.
- The payoff lands before the CTA slide. The last content slide is where "guilty it was free" is won or lost.
- Save-worthiness matters here more than anywhere. Frameworks, checklists, and step-by-steps score well on filters 2 and 4.
- Score the caption too, lightly. It should add context, not repeat the slides.

## Short video script (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts)

- **Hook** = the first two seconds, spoken line and on-screen text together. Both should hit, and they can say different things.
- Get to the point by second three. No "Hey guys" and no introductions.
- One idea per video. A payoff that comes after 45 seconds gets lost.
- Check the script reads naturally out loud. Written sentences sound stiff when spoken.
- Retention beats: a new piece of value or a visual change every few seconds.
- CTA is spoken or on screen at the end, short, and a single ask.

## Text post (LinkedIn, X, Threads, Facebook)

- **Hook** = the first one to two lines before "see more" (LinkedIn shows about 210 characters). On X, the whole post often is the hook.
- White space helps, but a post made entirely of single-line zingers reads as a formula.
- LinkedIn: professional context doesn't mean corporate voice. The reader-as-hero filter still applies. "I'm humbled to announce" posts score low on filter 5.
- X and Threads: density wins. Every word has to carry weight.

## Thread (X, Threads)

- **Hook** = post 1, and it must promise a clear payoff.
- Every post in the thread should stand on its own and push to the next one.
- The payoff comes before the final post. The last post is for the summary or CTA, not the main value.

## Story sequence (Instagram, Facebook stories)

- **Hook** = frame 1. It must make her tap forward instead of swiping away.
- Each frame earns the next tap. Three to seven frames is typical.
- Interactive stickers (polls, questions, sliders) can serve as the CTA and score well on filter 7 when they're easy and relevant.
- Stories are more casual, so a lower polish bar is fine. The value bar stays the same.

## When the format is unclear

Infer from structure (slide numbers mean carousel, "[on screen]" cues mean video script). State the assumption in the header: "Scoring as an Instagram carousel. Tell me if it's something else."
