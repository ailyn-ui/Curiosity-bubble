# Rewrite Playbook

Use this in Fix mode, and as the source for "directions" in Coach mode.

## Rule zero: never invent facts

Specificity raises every score, and that makes it tempting to make things up. Don't. No invented statistics, studies, percentages, client names, client results, testimonials, credentials, or quotes.

When a line needs a detail only the user has, write a bracketed placeholder and flag it after the post:
- "My client went from [starting number] to [result] in [timeframe]."
- "[Name the exact software you use]"

Specific and true beats specific and invented every time. A post that falsifies a result can cost someone their reputation, or break the law in regulated industries.

## Moves that raise each filter

**Stops the scroll**
- Put her identity, situation, or exact words in the first five words.
- Swap abstractions for a concrete moment: "Tuesday at 4pm when the invoice still isn't sent" beats "struggling with admin."
- Cut the warm-up. The first sentence that says something real is usually the third one. Start there.
- Promise only what the post delivers.

**Most valuable thing today**
- Add the mechanism: why the problem happens, not only what to do about it.
- Use the user's "what I know that others miss" from the profile. That's where the fresh angle lives.
- Replace every vague word with a number, a name, or a step.
- One idea, fully delivered, beats five ideas skimmed.

**Send to a friend**
- Name the exact moment, thought, or habit she's embarrassed to admit.
- Use the words she'd use, not industry language.

**Guilty it was free**
- Move the useful part above the CTA. If the how is being held back for the DM, give it away.
- Cut filler. If a sentence doesn't teach, validate, or move her forward, delete it. Aim to cut 20% of the words.

**Reader is the hero**
- Change "I" sentences to "you" sentences wherever the meaning holds.
- Keep creator stories, and end them on her: "If that's where you are, here's the first thing I'd change."

**Valuable without buying**
- Make sure a complete takeaway lands before the offer is mentioned.
- Make the offer the natural next step for someone who wants more of what they just got.

**CTA is earned**
- One ask. Delete the rest.
- Match the ask's size to the value delivered.
- Make it specific and easy: a word to comment, a link, a single question to answer.

## Sound like a person

A rewrite that scores 9 on value and reads like a chatbot still fails, because readers scroll past AI-sounding posts on sight. Every rewrite must use the user's voice from the profile and avoid these tells:

- **Filler and AI vocabulary:** delve, unpack, game-changer, journey, navigate, elevate, unlock, empower, tapestry, landscape, seamless, robust, resonate, "in today's fast-paced world."
- **Throat-clearing phrases:** "Here's the thing," "Let's be honest," "The truth is," "Let that sink in," "Read that again."
- **Formula structures:** "It's not X, it's Y" contrasts used over and over, stacks of one-line paragraphs, three punchy fragments in a row, a tidy one-line moral at the end.
- **Punctuation tells:** em dashes (use commas, periods, or line breaks instead), unless the user's own writing uses them.
- **Engagement bait:** "Sound familiar?", "Who else?", "Tag someone who needs this!", "Agree?"
- **Rhythm:** every sentence the same length. Mix short and long, and let some sentences run and connect with "and."

Read the rewrite back as if the user were saying it out loud to a friend. If it sounds written rather than said, fix it.

## Keep what's theirs

- Keep the user's best lines word for word when they already work.
- Keep their format (a carousel stays a carousel, same slide count unless the count is the problem).
- Keep their emoji, capitalization, and slang habits.
- Apply their "never say" list and any compliance limits.

## "What changed" notes

After a rewrite, list up to three moves in plain language so the user learns the pattern:
- "Moved your client result into the first line so the hook names the outcome."
- "Gave away the 3 steps instead of saving them for the DM."
- "Cut the opening two sentences about your week."
