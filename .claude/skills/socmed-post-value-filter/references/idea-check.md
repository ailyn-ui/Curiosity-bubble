# Idea Check

Most weak posts were weak ideas before anyone wrote a word. This check catches that in thirty seconds.

## The four criteria

**Specific (0-10)**
Is the idea narrow enough to deliver something usable in one post?
- 3/10: "Tips for better sleep"
- 8/10: "Why waking at 3am every night is a blood sugar problem, and the one snack that fixes it for most people"

**Fresh (0-10)**
Is this a take the reader hasn't already seen twenty times this month? Pull from the profile's "what I know that others miss." Fresh doesn't require being contrarian. It can be a familiar topic with a new mechanism, a new number, or a new moment.
- 3/10: "Why you need an email list"
- 8/10: "Your email list isn't dead. Your subject lines are. The 4-word test I run before every send."

**Her moment (0-10)**
Is the idea tied to a real moment in her life she'll recognize instantly?
- 3/10: "Improving team communication"
- 8/10: "The Slack message you send at 9pm that makes your team dread Monday"

**Fits the format (0-10)**
Can the full payoff land in one post in the intended format? Big topics crammed into one caption score low. Suggest a series or a different format when that's the fix.

## Verdicts

- **WRITE IT:** all four are 7 or higher.
- **SHARPEN IT:** any criterion is 5 or 6, nothing lower.
- **SKIP IT:** any criterion is 4 or below. Say what can't be saved in one line.

## Three sharper angles

Always give three, even when the verdict is WRITE IT. Each angle gets:

```
Angle 1: [short name]
Hook: "[a first line written for this reader]"
The takeaway: [the one thing she'll know or be able to do after]
Best format: [carousel / reel / text post / thread / story sequence] · [why, in a few words]
```

Make the three angles different from each other. A good spread: one teaches a mechanism, one names a mistake, one tells a story that mirrors her. Never invent stats or client results inside an angle. Use placeholders if needed.

End with: "Draft whichever one pulls you and paste it back. I'll score it."
