# The 12 Hook Patterns

These patterns show up again and again in high-performing short-form content across creators and industries. Each one triggers a different reaction, which is why a batch needs variety.

**About the examples:** every example below is illustrative and was written for this guide. The numbers and stories are placeholders showing the shape of the pattern. Never reuse them as if they were the user's proof.

**Needs proof:** patterns 1-4 need real material from the user's "My real proof." Patterns 5-12 need none.

---

## 1. Start-Over Plan

**Requires:** the user has achieved the outcome, for themselves or for clients.
**Formula:** "If I had to [reach outcome] from zero in [timeframe], here's exactly what I'd do." or "If I lost [key asset] tomorrow, these are the first [number] moves I'd make."
**Why it works:** it removes the "easy for you to say" objection. The reader builds her own plan alongside yours.
**Best for:** reach, shares, and teaching a process.
**Examples:**
- "If I had to fill a new yoga studio from zero in 90 days, here's the exact order I'd do it in."
- "If my agency lost every client tomorrow, this is the first email I'd send on Monday."

**Don't use if:** the user hasn't done the thing they'd be "redoing."

---

## 2. Low Point

**Requires:** a real personal story the user is willing to share.
**Formula:** "[The moment, stated plainly]. It was [specific time or place]. [The detail that made it worse]."
**Why it works:** vulnerability is rare in a feed full of highlight reels, and specific details (a date, a place, a dollar amount) make it believable.
**Best for:** connection, trust, and making a later result feel earned.
**Examples:**
- "The day I closed my first salon, I had $212 in the business account and a lease with 14 months left."
- "I cried in the parking lot after my first sales call. They said yes. I'd quoted half my costs."

**Don't use if:** the story doesn't lead to a lesson for the reader. A low point with no point reads as a bid for sympathy.

---

## 3. Before/After

**Requires:** a real result, the user's own or a client's (with permission).
**Formula:** "[Subject] went from [specific before] to [specific after]. Here's what changed." or "Before: [metric]. After: [metric]. One change."
**Why it works:** contrast is instant story. The reader wants the bridge between the two states.
**Best for:** authority, proof, and leading into a tactical breakdown.
**Examples:**
- "This client's week went from 62 hours to 41. We didn't hire anyone."
- "Same product, same price. Conversion went from 1.2% to 3.8% after we rewrote one section of the page."

---

## 4. Credential Bridge

**Requires:** a verifiable credential or number (years, clients, revenue, qualifications, reviews examined).
**Formula:** "[Biggest true number]. That's [what it means]. Here's [the lesson, system, or mistake]." Variant: "I reviewed [number] [things] and [the pattern I found]."
**Why it works:** the number earns the right to teach, and the pivot to value keeps people past the flex.
**Best for:** authority and introducing yourself to new audiences.
**Examples:**
- "I've done the taxes for over 300 contractors. The ones who keep the most money all do this in January."
- "I read every one-star review our competitors got last year. Three complaints came up again and again."

**Tip:** one strong credential can front many posts. Keep the credential and rotate the lesson.

---

## 5. Wrong Answer

**Requires:** a contrarian take or a mechanism (from "What I know that others miss").
**Formula:** "[Question with an obvious answer]? [The obvious answer]. Wrong. [The real answer]." or "Everyone says [common advice]. It's why [bad outcome]."
**Why it works:** the brain predicts what comes next. When the prediction breaks, attention spikes.
**Best for:** reframing beliefs, saves, and shares.
**Examples:**
- "Why do new clients ghost after the first session? Price, right? Wrong. It's what happens in the 48 hours after they pay."
- "Everyone says post every day. It's why your best content gets buried under your worst."

**Don't use if:** the "wrong" answer isn't wrong, or the real answer isn't surprising.

---

## 6. Signs You're

**Requires:** expertise only.
**Formula:** "Signs you're [doing X wrong / ready for Y]: [the first, most vivid sign]." or "You're [problem] if [specific behavior]."
**Why it works:** it invites self-assessment. People keep reading to see if the signs apply to them.
**Best for:** self-identification and repeatable content.
**Examples:**
- "Signs you've outgrown your bookkeeper: you find out your margins from your accountant in April."
- "You're overcomplicating your offer if you need more than one breath to explain it."

---

## 7. Mistake Warning

**Requires:** expertise only.
**Formula:** "Stop [common action] if you want [desired outcome]." or "The [thing] mistake that's costing you [specific cost]."
**Why it works:** people work harder to avoid a loss than to chase a gain.
**Best for:** leads, conversion, and tactical content.
**Examples:**
- "Stop sending proposals as PDFs if you want them read the same day."
- "The pricing-page mistake that makes buyers pick your cheapest option."

---

## 8. Rating

**Requires:** opinions and expertise.
**Formula:** "[Tactic or tool]: [number] out of 10. [One-line reason]." Series title: "[Your role] rates [category] from 1 to 10."
**Why it works:** every viewer rates along with you, and disagreement drives comments.
**Best for:** comments, debate, and covering many topics fast.
**Examples:**
- "Batch cooking on Sundays: 4 out of 10. Here's what my clients do instead."
- "A realtor rates home-staging tips from 1 to 10. Fresh flowers: 2."

---

## 9. Honest Admission

**Requires:** nothing. Works well for people without big credentials.
**Formula:** "[Admit a weakness]. [Admit another]. But [the thing you're great at or committed to]."
**Why it works:** leading with a negative defies the pattern of self-promotion, so the positive that follows is easier to believe.
**Best for:** trust and lowering resistance before an offer.
**Examples:**
- "I'm not the cheapest photographer in town. I'm not the fastest. But no client of mine has ever had to ask where their photos are."
- "I've never gone viral. My list is 800 people. It made more than my last job did."

(The second example needs real numbers from the user. Use placeholders if you don't have them.)

---

## 10. Bold Declaration

**Requires:** a point of view.
**Formula:** one punchy, quotable statement, under 15 words, with no buildup.
**Why it works:** it works like a billboard. People who agree feel validated and people who disagree feel provoked, and both engage.
**Best for:** saves, shares, and brand building.
**Rules:** one idea, specific to the niche, and short enough for a t-shirt. Generic motivation fails ("Believe in yourself" could come from anyone).
**Examples:**
- "Your menu has too many items, and your food cost proves it."
- "A client who needs convincing is a refund waiting to happen."

---

## 11. Her Exact Words

**Requires:** customer language (the "Her words" line in the profile, or pasted DMs, reviews, or transcripts).
**Formula:** open with a real phrase a client said, in quotes or as your own first line, then turn it. "'[Exact client phrase].' I hear this every week. Here's what's going on." or use the phrase directly as the hook.
**Why it works:** the reader hears her own inner monologue. Nothing feels more written-for-her than her own words.
**Best for:** connection, conversion, and ads.
**Examples:**
- "'I know what to do, I just don't do it.' If that's you, the problem isn't discipline."
- "'I feel like I'm always behind on my books.' You're not behind. Your system is built for someone with more hours than you."

**Note:** strip names and identifying details. Confirm the quote is real, and never invent one.

---

## 12. Hot Seat

**Requires:** a real client situation (anonymized, with permission), or a scenario clearly framed as hypothetical.
**Formula:** "[Business type], [key detail], wants [goal]. Here's the first thing I'd change." or "Say you run a [business] doing [range]. Here's where your money's leaking."
**Why it works:** it feels like overhearing a private coaching session, and every viewer swaps in her own situation.
**Best for:** comments, showing expertise, and community engagement.
**Examples:**
- "Dog groomer, two vans, fully booked, still not profitable. Here's the first thing I'd look at."
- "Say you're a therapist with a 40-person waitlist. Raising your rate is the obvious move, and it's the wrong first move."

**Don't:** present a made-up scenario as a real client conversation. Use "Say you're..." framing for hypotheticals.

---

## Stacking for stronger hooks

Once a batch has variety, the best hooks often combine two triggers in one sentence:

- **Pain + contrarian:** "You're working weekends because you're fixing the wrong bottleneck."
- **Proof + curiosity:** "[Real result], and it had nothing to do with [the thing everyone assumes]."
- **Her words + mechanism:** "'I just need more leads.' You don't. You need the 30% who already inquired to answer back."

## What makes a hook land

A strong hook usually uses at least two of these:

| Ingredient | What it does |
|---|---|
| Curiosity | Breaks the reader's prediction, so she thinks "wait, what?" |
| Specificity | Numbers, dates, places, and names make it real. |
| Stakes | Something is on the line: money, time, relationships, reputation. |
| Tension | A contrast, a disagreement, or an unresolved question. |
| Clear value | She knows what she'll get and what shape it arrives in. |

## Common ways hooks fail

Flag these when you see them in a user's hook:

1. **Generic motivation.** "The biggest opportunities hide where you're most afraid to look." It could come from anyone, so nobody stops.
2. **No specifics.** Nothing to picture or measure.
3. **Slow start.** "So, um, I think..." or "Many of you probably remember..."
4. **Buried hook.** The interesting part is in sentence two or three.
5. **Weak verbs.** "Is," "was," and "can be" where "kills," "costs," or "builds" belong.
6. **Written without customer language.** It ends up sounding like marketing.
7. **Clickbait.** The post doesn't pay off what the hook promised, which trains people to skip you.
8. **Virality over fit.** A hook that stops 10,000 of the wrong people is worth less than one that stops 500 of the right ones.
