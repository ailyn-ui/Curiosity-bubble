# Idea scoring rubric

Score each idea from 1 to 5 on six criteria. Each criterion is about the
audience and the market, never about the founder's skills or background.

| # | Criterion | Weight | 1 means | 5 means |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|---------|
| 1 | **Demand evidence** | ×3 | one or two mentions | the pain repeats across many sources and source types |
| 2 | **Willingness to pay** | ×3 | they expect it free | similar products sell at this price with many reviews |
| 3 | **Gap** | ×2 | well-served by good products | the existing options are clearly criticized, missing or outdated |
| 4 | **Reachability as a new brand** | ×2 | no open place to reach them | active communities, search traffic or marketplaces where a newcomer can show up |
| 5 | **Speed to a first sellable version** | ×1 | months, or needs expert credentials or licensing | sellable in days or a couple of weeks, with research and AI help |
| 6 | **Low risk** | ×1 | regulated advice (medical, legal, financial), or high liability | no special regulatory or safety exposure |

**Total = weighted sum, out of 60.** Show the table sorted by total.

Notes:
- Base each score on the research log and cite the evidence lines in a short
  "why" column. If you had to guess, mark the score with `?`.
- Criterion 5 asks how fast a sellable version can exist, not whether the
  user can build it. Research, AI drafting, templates and freelancers all
  count.
- Criterion 6 exists so that ideas in regulated areas are flagged, not
  quietly dropped. If one scores well anyway, say what it would need, for
  example review by a qualified professional or a clear disclaimer.
- A tie or near-tie (within 3 points) is a judgment call: prefer the idea
  with stronger **money** evidence.
