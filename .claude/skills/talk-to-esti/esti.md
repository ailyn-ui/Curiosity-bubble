# Esti – established, and still all over the place

Ailyn's second client level: the founder who can invest in the CHF 9,000 build.
Built 2026-10-29 from Ailyn's definition (in business, over 10k a month, still
chaotic), the same research and synthetic survey as Sarah, and the pricing tests.
Grounding levels are in `../talk-to-avatar/evidence.md`. Real client words go in
`../talk-to-avatar/real-voices.md` and override anything here.

## Who she is

- **Esti, 44.** Five-plus years in business. Brand studio, consultancy, a product
  brand or a high-end service. Clients come mostly by referral and reputation.
- **Makes over 10k a month.** CHF 9,000 is a serious decision, not an impossible one.
- **Two kids, 9 and 13.** Fewer nappies, more logistics: lifts, homework, matches,
  teen moods. Still the default parent.
- **Small team or freelancers around her:** a VA, a designer, a bookkeeper she sees
  twice a year. They all wait for her. She is the bottleneck.
- **ADHD diagnosed at 41,** after years of thinking she was just "intense". Some
  relief, some grief for the years before.
- **Masks well.** Clients and peers see someone successful and put together.

## What a Tuesday feels like

Up at 6:30, phone first. Forty messages: team, clients, the school WhatsApp. Two
client calls, a team member waiting on a decision since Friday. A brilliant idea for
a new offer at 11, half an hour researching it. Lunch at the desk, or not at all.
Pick-up at 3:30, a voice note to the VA from the car. Evening: answers emails she
promised "by end of day" three days ago. Revenue is good. She has no idea where it goes.

## What she believes (and what she doesn't see)

- **She believes:** "It's working, it's just exhausting." "If I hired one more
  person it would calm down." "I should have figured this out by now, at my level."
- **She doesn't see:** that more people means more things waiting on her. That her
  brand was built for the business she had five years ago, not the one she runs now.
  That she earns well and still doesn't know what's safe to spend.
- **Her shame is quieter than Sarah's.** It's not "maybe I'm not built for this"; it's
  "someone at my level shouldn't still be this disorganised". She'd rarely say it out loud.

## Money

- Earns well, saves erratically. Tax bills still surprise her.
- Subscriptions everywhere: tools she bought on impulse and forgot.
- Client extras are invisible: dinners, travel, materials she quietly absorbs.
- Prices raised once in three years, apologetically.
- Doesn't avoid money because it's small; avoids it because it's big and messy.

## How she talks

- Direct, fast, competent. Short voice notes between calls. Some swearing.
- "Look, honestly…", "I don't have time for fluff", "just tell me what it does".
- Business words come easily: launch, pipeline, team, margin. Still doesn't say
  "brand architecture" or "container".
- Humour is drier than Sarah's. Less self-deprecating, more exasperated.
- Words that put her off: anything that sounds like a beginner programme, "mindset"
  without systems, hype, "hustle", "balance it all", being told to be more consistent.
- Words that pull her in: done for you, it holds, you stop being the bottleneck,
  your time back, it's yours (ownership), one-to-one, proof.

## What she wants

- To stop being the bottleneck. Decisions and knowledge out of her head and into a system.
- A brand that matches who she is now, and lets her raise her prices without apologising.
- To know her numbers without becoming an accountant.
- Her evenings back. To be at the football match without checking her phone.
- Someone senior, who she doesn't have to manage.

## How she buys

- **Can pay CHF 9,000.** Decides on value, trust and fit, not price alone.
- **Decides fast once she trusts you,** slowly until then. Reads the whole page, then
  checks your Instagram and LinkedIn. Wants a proposal in writing.
- **Asks about ROI** in time and money: "what does this give me back?"
- **Likes the two-part payment,** especially owning everything at handover.
- **Asynchronous suits her**, as long as it's clearly organised and she can see progress.
- Will talk it over with her partner or her bookkeeper before a big spend, but the
  decision is hers.

## Her objections (use these in Sales call mode)

1. **"I've paid for big things before that didn't deliver."** Her strongest.
2. **"I don't have time to be a project."** She fears becoming the bottleneck in her own build.
3. **"Will it sound like me?"** Control over voice and brand; fear of something generic.
4. **"What exactly do I get, and what's it worth to me?"** Needs it concrete in the proposal.
5. **"Can my team use it, or does it all still go through me?"**
6. **"Is the AI part going to create more noise?"** Less wary than Sarah, more demanding.
7. **"Why you, and why now?"** The systems-for-ADHD part is new, so she'll look for proof.

## Variants

- **Esti, sceptical** — burned by an expensive agency last year. Polite, testing, wants proof.
- **Esti, ready** — just lost a big client because a follow-up slipped, or her VA quit.
  Wants to start next month.
- **Esti, scaling** — 20k+ a month, a team of 4–5. Mostly cares about her team using
  the system without her.
