# Brand: the shared workbook for a mother and her almost-grown child

A stand-alone brand. Nothing here borrows the user's consultancy name, voice, palette or offers, and none of its files were read for style. The voice below comes from how the audience talks in the research files.

## 1. Name

The product is one workbook, so the brand and the product use **one name** (with a subtitle). Fewer names means less to build and remember.

| # | Name | Style | Clash check (web search, 2026-11-01) | Notes |
|---|------|-------|----------------------------------------|-------|
| 1 | **The Hinge Years** | evocative | No brand, book or podcast found under this exact name ([search](https://instituteofcoaching.org/resources/chip-conley-learning-love-midlife-how-coach-people-through-life-stage)). The space is full of "Midlife ___" names, so this stands out | A hinge is the point on which **both** sides turn, and it's what opens a door. It fits her stage *and* theirs |
| 2 | Two Crossroads | descriptive | No exact match, **but** "Crossroads" is heavily used by Christian counselling and coaching ([crossroadcounselor](https://crossroadcounselor.com/life-coaching/)) and by churches. It clashes with the secular positioning and would be hard to find in search | Good working title, risky as a brand |
| 3 | Side by Side | descriptive | A very common phrase. Some career workbooks already use "side-by-side" sections ([search results](https://www.montcalm.edu/news/book-helps-parents-guide-teens-in-exploring-career-path/)). Hard to own | Clear meaning, weak to own |
| 4 | Both Ways | evocative | No product found. **Risk:** the idiom "swings both ways" | Drop |
| 5 | The Kitchen Table | evocative | No product found, but search is swamped by furniture and cookbooks | Warm, hard to find online |
| 6 | Ask, Don't Steer | descriptive | Not checked as a brand; it works better as a **tagline** | Captures the method |
| 7 | Next & Next | invented | Not checked | Playful; meaning not obvious |

**Recommendation: The Hinge Years**, with the subtitle *"A workbook for a mother and her almost-grown child, deciding what's next together."*
Tagline options: *"Two decisions. Neither made for the other."* / *"Ask, don't steer."*

**Before you spend money:**
- I can't check domains or social handles from this session. Check `thehingeyears.com`, `.co` and the Instagram, TikTok and Pinterest handles yourself.
- Run a proper trademark search (USPTO, EUIPO, Swissreg, and UK IPO if you sell in the UK). A web search isn't a trademark search.

**Products in the same format:** mother–child interactive journals exist, such as *Just You & Me: An Interactive Journal for Mother and Son* ([bookshop](https://bookshop.rabata.org/products/just-you-me-an-interactive-journal-for-mother-and-son)). They're about bonding, not decisions. That proves the "two people, one book" format sells, and the decision angle stays ours.

## 2. Positioning

**For** mothers in their late 40s and 50s whose child (18–20) is deciding what to study, **who** are quietly asking "what's next for me?" at the same time. **The Hinge Years is** a shared workbook that gets both of you to a decision in five sittings. **Unlike** career tests (which stop at "your type"), parenting guides (which treat her only as the helper) and empty-nest journals (which stay with feelings), **it** has both people make their own decision side by side, so she supports their choice instead of steering it, and stops putting off her own.

**In one line:** *Two people at a crossroads, one workbook, two decisions.*

## 3. Voice

Taken from how the audience talks in the research: plain words ("lost", "stuck", "no idea", "who am I now", "what's next"), mild humour, tired of fluff.

| Trait | Sounds like | Doesn't sound like |
|---|---|---|
| **Fair to both** | "This page is yours. Theirs is on the right. Don't read it unless they hand it to you." | "Moms, here's how to guide your teen to the right choice." |
| **Plain and practical** | "Write down three fields. Then find one person who works in each." | "Unlock your limitless potential and embrace the journey." |
| **Calm about the uncertainty** | "Not knowing yet is normal. You'll know more after sitting 3." | "Don't panic! You've got this, mama!" |
| **Lightly funny** | "Yes, you also get a page. No, you can't skip it." | Sarcasm about the kid, or about her age |

**Use:** decide, try, ask, choose, next, together, your page / their page, sitting.
**Avoid:** journey, unlock, empower, thrive, embrace, manifest, mama, "empty nest syndrome", "AI-proof" (say "AI-resilient" and explain it), religious framing, therapy language ("trauma", "healing"). Always use they/them for the child, so the workbook fits sons, daughters and non-binary kids.

**Required lines (from the research):**
- "This workbook is not a substitute for professional help. If either of you is struggling with anxiety or low mood, please talk to a doctor or counsellor."
- On money and careers: "General information, not financial or career advice."

## 4. Look

The idea: **the open spread.** One page for her, one for them, and the hinge (the spine) in the middle. Two colours, one per person, that meet in the middle.

| Role | Colour | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | warm off-white | `#F6F1E8` | background |
| Ink | deep slate | `#23303A` | all body text (high contrast on paper) |
| Her colour | terracotta | `#C0603A` | her page tabs, large headings only |
| Their colour | deep teal | `#2F6F6B` | their page tabs, large headings only |
| Hinge | sand | `#E3D3B8` | the centre line, shared-conversation boxes |
| Accent | soft ochre | `#D9A441` | small highlights, at most one per page |

Body text stays in ink. Terracotta and teal are for large headings, tabs and shapes, so the pages stay easy to read.

**Fonts** (both free from Google Fonts, licensed for commercial use):
- Headings: **Fraunces**, a warm serif with personality
- Body and worksheets: **Source Sans 3**, clean and easy to read in small sizes and on print

**Mood:** a well-made notebook on a kitchen table. Paper texture, lots of white space, hand-drawn lines and arrows, no stock photos of smiling families. One simple icon: two lines meeting at a hinge point.

**Formats:** A4/Letter PDF (printable and fillable), with the cover designed so print-on-demand (Amazon KDP or Lulu) can come later without a redesign.
