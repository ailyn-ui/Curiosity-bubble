## Recommendation: Two Crossroads

**The promise:** *"Your kid is deciding what to study. You're deciding what's next for you. Do it side by side, in six weeks, without steering their choice or putting off yours."*

**Structure:** each week, a track for her, a track for them, and one 30-minute conversation together.

| Week | Their track (from the Sprint) | Her track (from the Next Chapter Plan) | Shared conversation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What I like, what I'm good at, what I'm curious about | Who am I now? What did I put on hold? | "What did you want at 19? What do I want now?" |
| 2 | Shortlist 3 fields | Mothering an adult: what changes | "What kind of help do you actually want from me?" |
| 3 | Test-drive field 1–2 (mini-projects, an informational interview) | Work and purpose: one small experiment | Swap what each of you learned |
| 4 | Test-drive field 3, plus an AI-resilience check | Marriage, or life on your own; money basics (education only) | "What scares each of us about the next 5 years?" |
| 5 | Narrow down to 1–2 | Narrow down to 1–2 directions | Each presents a choice; the other only asks questions |
| 6 | The decision, plus a one-page brief | Her ten-year plan on one page | "Here's my plan. Here's how you can support it." |

**Why the format works:**
- The mother doing her own work is the antidote to steering (see the research).
- One buyer gets two outcomes, which makes the higher price easier to justify.
- The shared conversations are the part people will talk about, and the reason to recommend it to friends.

**Ladder:** card deck C ($29) as the entry product → Two Crossroads ($149) → later, an optional live workshop D.

## Marketing angle: lead with the urgent pain

She'll search for and buy help for her child first. Run 2 suggests she puts her kids first. So:
- **The hook:** "Your 18 year old doesn't know what to study (and maybe you don't know what's next either)."
- **The free lead magnet:** *"The 5 conversations to have before they choose a major"* (a PDF), plus the 10-question AI-resilience check for the young adult.
- **Channels:** Grown and Flown and parent groups (be helpful within their rules, no link drops), midlife and parenting podcasts, Pinterest, and school counsellors as partners.

## Validation test (2 weeks)

1. A waitlist page with a founding pre-sale at $99, using the lead magnet above.
2. **Run a split test:** headline 1 leads with the child, headline 2 leads with her. This answers the "will she buy for herself?" question with data.
3. **Pass if:** at least 200 sign-ups and at least 10 pre-sales. If headline 2 wins, the market is bigger than expected, and The Next Chapter Plan could stand alone later.

## Notes

- **Overlap with your own audience:** yes, it's a neighbouring audience (mothers, some of them founders). The separate brand keeps it apart. You've said you're comfortable with that.
- Every mention of money, menopause or mental health stays educational, with pointers to professional support.
- Young adults are 18+ and take part voluntarily. Their track should stand on its own, so it still works if the mother drops out (or the other way round).

**Checkpoint:** pick A, or another idea, then we go to phase 3: the brand.
